01 — SkillMatch

Helping people recognise transferable skills and explore explainable career possibilities.

8High-fidelity prototype screens
2Formative test participants
4Sample career paths

The challenge

As AI changes jobs, workers may struggle to translate the work they already do into other career opportunities. Job titles alone can conceal useful transferable capabilities.

My approach

01 · EvidenceStart with real tasks and work experience.
02 · InterpretSuggest capabilities, clearly labelled as AI interpretations.
03 · ConfirmAllow users to edit or reject suggestions.
04 · ExplainShow career matches, rationale, gaps and next steps.

What testing changed

Two users saw value in discovering unfamiliar career paths, but found some explanations information-heavy. One user initially struggled to understand why UX Researcher was recommended. I revised the explanation flow to lead with the conclusion and then reveal the evidence supporting it.

Scope and honesty: This is a student prototype using sample data, not a deployed AI career service. Testing with two users is formative and does not establish employment outcomes or broad market validity.

02 — TRACE

Making recurring bank payments and subscriptions easier to understand and manage.

4Qualitative research interviews
2Banking competitors examined
ZARSouth African rand context

The challenge

Recurring payments can be difficult to identify, monitor and manage. Users may encounter unfamiliar merchant descriptions, unexpected deductions or subscriptions they no longer need.

Research and synthesis

I interviewed four people, compared FNB and Standard Bank experiences, organised potential solutions using affinity mapping, and evaluated features through an impact-versus-effort matrix.

Design direction

VisibilityConsolidated recurring payment overview.
UnderstandingClearer merchant and payment information.
AwarenessUpcoming payment reminders.
ControlGuidance to the correct bank or merchant action.

Illustrative ZAR interface content

What I learned

The opportunity is broader than simply cancelling subscriptions: visibility, understandable merchant information and appropriately guided management actions matter. TRACE would not independently reverse or cancel transactions; it would direct users to the relevant authorised process.

Project stage: Research, analysis and feature prioritisation are documented in FigJam. Mobile interface screens and a tested TRACE prototype are future work, not completed deliverables.
